# Nomad Design Direction

## Atlas Control Room

- **Product mode:** Operate. Nomad is a recurring residency-operations tool,
  not a travel diary or destination-discovery product.
- **Audience and cadence:** Frequent international travellers reviewing
  sensitive presence, visa, tax-residency, and immigration records weekly and
  before consequential trips.
- **Visual world:** Atlas Control Room — calm, exact, private, and prepared.
  The interface should feel like a trusted instrument panel, not an adventure
  brand.
- **Palette family:** Fog and graphite neutrals with one restrained deep-petrol
  accent and a cobalt information accent. Green, amber, and red are reserved
  for compliance meaning: safe, warning, and danger.
- **Type treatment:** Sans-first Inter hierarchy, compact labels, tabular data,
  and deliberate weight rather than decorative display type.
- **Composition:** A dense, scannable operations cockpit with quiet chrome,
  strong alignment, and progressive disclosure for detail.
- **Shape language:** Crisp geometry, quiet corners, fine dividers, and the
  Agent Native mark in the app shell. Shadows are shallow and functional.
- **Motion:** Brief state transitions only. Looping status motion must respect
  reduced-motion preferences.

## Anti-references

- Travel-blog photography, passport-stamp nostalgia, or playful tourism motifs
- Generic purple AI gradients, glass panels, sparkle icons, or inflated hero copy
- Green used as product branding rather than a positive compliance status
- Equal-weight card grids, chip-heavy summaries, and explanatory first viewports
- Developer diagnostics competing with the core Cockpit, Agent, and Settings routes

## Review Standard

The first viewport should answer three questions without explanation: where the
traveller is, which thresholds need attention, and what action is next. The
left rail stays product-focused in App mode; framework diagnostics appear only
in Code mode. Light, dark, narrow, focus, loading, empty, and error states must
retain the same restrained hierarchy.
